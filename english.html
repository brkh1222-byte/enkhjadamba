02Building a sentence

Subject + Verb + Object → Enkh bakes a cake.
  • A sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with . ? !
  • Questions often flip the order: Does Enkh bake cakes?
  • Join two ideas with and, but, so, because: I was tired, but I finished the problem.

03Tenses in one table

Using the verb bake:

PastPresentFuture
SimpleI bakedI bakeI will bake
ContinuousI was bakingI am bakingI will be baking
PerfectI had bakedI have bakedI will have baked
Perfect continuousI had been bakingI have been bakingI will have been baking
Simple = facts & habits · Continuous = in progress right then · Perfect = finished before a time · Perfect continuous = how long it has been going on

04a / an / the

  • a before a consonant sound: a cake, a university (sounds like "you")
  • an before a vowel sound: an apple, an hour (the h is silent)
  • the = a specific one we both know: Pass me the cake you baked.
  • No article for general plurals: Cakes are delicious.

05Subject–verb agreement

One subject → verb with -s. Many subjects → no -s.
  • She bakes · She bake
  • They bake · They bakes
  • Each of the students has a book. (each is singular)
  • Math and English are fun. (two things joined by and = plural)

Long words (2+ syllables, not ending in -y) use more / most. Never both: more easier.

07in / on / at (time)

Think of a funnel: in = big, on = medium, at = exact point.
  • in + months, years, seasons: in May, in 2026, in winter
  • on + days and dates: on Monday, on 5 June, on my birthday
  • at + clock times: at 8:30, at noon, at night

08Punctuation basics

  • Comma in lists: I need flour, sugar, eggs and butter.
  • Comma after an intro: After school, I practise math.
  • Apostrophe for owning: Enkh's cake (one owner), the students' books (many owners)
  • Apostrophe for short forms: do not → don't, it is → it's
