00How to attack a problem

  1. Read it twice. Underline what is given and what is asked.
  2. Try small cases. If it says 100, try 1, 2, 3, 4 first and look for a pattern.
  3. Draw a picture or make a table.
  4. Ask: which tool fits? Odd/even? Remainders? Pigeonhole? Counting?
  5. Check your answer by plugging it back in.

01Parity (odd & even)

even ± even = even · odd ± odd = even · even ± odd = odd
A sum is odd exactly when it has an odd number of odd terms.

Parity is great for proving something is impossible.

Problem. Can you pick 5 odd numbers that add up to 50?
Show answer
No. Five odd numbers is an odd number of odd terms, so the sum is odd. 50 is even.

Problem. Find the digit A so that 4A72 is divisible by 9.
Show answer
4 + A + 7 + 2 = 13 + A must be a multiple of 9, so A = 5. Check: 4572 = 9 × 508. ✓

03Primes, GCD & LCM

Every whole number > 1 splits into primes in exactly one way: 60 = 2² × 3 × 5.
GCD = biggest number dividing both. LCM = smallest number both divide.
GCD(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b

Use GCD for "split into equal groups". Use LCM for "when do things line up again".

Problem. Bus A leaves every 12 minutes and bus B every 18 minutes. Both leave at 8:00. When do they next leave together?
Show answer
12 = 2²·3, 18 = 2·3², so LCM = 2²·3² = 36. They meet again at 8:36.

04Remainders (modular arithmetic)

a ≡ b (mod n) means a and b leave the same remainder when divided by n.
You can add, subtract and multiply remainders instead of the huge numbers.

Trick: sometimes a remainder of n−1 is easier to think of as −1.

Problem. What is the remainder when 2¹⁰⁰ is divided by 3?
Show answer
2 ≡ −1 (mod 3), so 2¹⁰⁰ ≡ (−1)¹⁰⁰ = 1. The remainder is 1.

05Last-digit patterns

Last digits of powers repeat in short cycles:
2 → 2,4,8,6 · 3 → 3,9,7,1 · 7 → 7,9,3,1 · 8 → 8,4,2,6
Find where you are in the cycle using the remainder of the exponent.
Problem. What is the last digit of 7²⁰²⁶?
Show answer
The cycle 7, 9, 3, 1 has length 4. 2026 = 4 × 506 + 2, so we are at position 2: the last digit is 9.

06Pigeonhole principle

If you put more than n pigeons into n holes, some hole gets at least 2 pigeons.

Example: among any 13 people, two were born in the same month (13 people, 12 months).

Problem. A dark drawer has red, blue and green socks. How many socks must you take to be sure you have a matching pair?
Show answer
4. With 3 socks you might get one of each colour. The 4th sock must match one of them (3 colours = 3 holes).

07Counting

Product rule: if step 1 has a choices and step 2 has b, together there are a × b.
Pairs: the number of ways to pick 2 from n is n(n−1)/2.
Problem 1. How many 3-digit numbers have only odd digits?
Show answer
Each digit has 5 choices (1, 3, 5, 7, 9): 5 × 5 × 5 = 125.
Problem 2. 10 friends each shake hands with every other friend once. How many handshakes?
Show answer
10 × 9 / 2 = 45. (Divide by 2 because A–B and B–A are the same handshake.)

08Clever sums

Gauss's trick: pair first + last. 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n+1)/2
Sum of the first n odd numbers is n².

Example: 1 + 2 + … + 100 = 100 × 101 / 2 = 5050.

Problem. Find 1 + 3 + 5 + … + 99.
Show answer
There are 50 odd numbers from 1 to 99, so the sum is 50² = 2500.

09Invariants

An invariant is something that never changes, no matter which moves you make. Find it, and you can prove which endings are impossible.
Problem. The numbers 1, 2, …, 10 are on a board. A move: erase any two numbers a, b and write |a − b|. After 9 moves one number is left. Can it be 0?
Show answer
Each move changes the total by a + b − |a − b| = 2·min(a,b), an even number. So the parity of the total never changes. The total starts at 55 (odd), so the final number is odd. It can't be 0.

10Working backwards

If you know the end, undo each step in reverse order: + becomes −, × becomes ÷.
Problem. I think of a number, double it, add 6, then divide by 4. I get 5. What was my number?
Show answer
5 × 4 = 20, 20 − 6 = 14, 14 ÷ 2 = 7. The number is 7.

11Angles

Straight line = 180° · Triangle angles sum to 180° · Quadrilateral = 360° · Polygon with n sides = (n − 2) × 180°
Problem. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. What is the largest angle?
Show answer
2 + 3 + 4 = 9 parts = 180°, so one part is 20°. Largest: 4 × 20° = 80°.
